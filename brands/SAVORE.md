### SAVORÉ Table — contemporary Filipino restaurant identity / cultural pattern system

**Category:** Food & Beverage · Contemporary Filipino restaurant  
**Positioning:** A seat at our table. A proudly Filipino, generous, contemporary dining brand rooted in shared meals, familiar flavors, regional food references, and welcoming hospitality.

**Color system:** 60% Rice `#F8EFDC` · 30% Charcoal `#292521` · 10% Chili `#B83A2B`. Calamansi Gold `#F2BE4B` and Pandan Green `#466A4B` are supporting accents. Keep the food photography naturally colorful; use the graphic palette consistently.

**Typography:** Archivo Black (large menu/display headlines) · Plus Jakarta Sans (body, menu descriptions, CTA) · IBM Plex Mono (dish numbers, prices, order codes). Use a bold contemporary sans system rather than a luxury serif identity.

**Cultural graphic language:** Build a recognizable motif library using carefully researched, contemporary interpretations of banig-inspired geometric weave, hablon-inspired stripe rhythm, solihiya/rattan geometry, tablecloth checks, woven placemat borders, and hand-painted market/menu graphics. Patterns should frame information, create borders, or become vector backgrounds—not be pasted randomly over food photography. Do not use sacred, ceremonial, or community-specific motifs without context and permission. Avoid generic flag graphics and tourist clichés.

**Content language:** Use natural Filipino, English, and light Taglish where appropriate. Dish names and food language should be specific and familiar (e.g., kare-kare, chicken inasal, sinigang, inihaw, atsara, bagoong) without forced slang. Keep menu descriptions appetizing and straightforward.

**Voice:** Warm, generous, expressive, proudly Filipino, and hospitable. Make sharing and group dining visible in the content.

**Distinctness rule:** The first impression should be Filipino menu culture, bold type, rice/chili/calamansi colors, and authentic woven-pattern geometry—not a generic upscale restaurant still life.
